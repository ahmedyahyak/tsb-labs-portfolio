Case / 01 In daily use TSB Labs product

The company that sells an AI operating system is run by one

A solo founder running several ventures has the same problem every small company has, only sharper: the work of a sales team, an operations team and a support desk all exists, and none of the salaries do. The usual answers are to hire ahead of revenue, which kills companies, or to do everything personally, which caps them.

Madar OS is our answer, and TSB Labs is its first deployment. One brain with a persistent memory graph of the business, a bench of specialist roles it puts on as needed, and runtime search across a library of skills it looks up rather than installs. Every sensitive action passes a permission ring before it happens.

What runs without anyone watching

  • An inbound lane. A new enquiry is detected, researched against the memory graph, answered in the founder's voice, and written back to the CRM with its status changed.
  • A LinkedIn co pilot. It researches targets and drafts the messages, then stops. It never touches the platform itself, because LinkedIn's terms say a human sends. That boundary is written into the system, not left to discipline.
  • A doctor. Every five minutes it checks the other services and revives anything that has died.
  • A nightly backup with a restore drill that has been run rather than assumed, loading the dump into a throwaway database so the live one is never touched.

Case / 02 Live TSB Labs venture

A surplus food marketplace where the vendor pays nothing

Every night, venues across Muscat throw away food that was good an hour earlier. Customers would happily buy it at a discount. The reason the trade does not happen by itself is friction on the vendor's side: marketplaces take a commission on already discounted stock, and any system that needs staff training or new hardware at the counter dies in its first week.

Luqma removes both objections. Venues list deals at forty to eighty percent off, on their own schedule, whenever they know what is left. Members find them in the app and redeem with a single Wallet pass at the counter. There is no commission for vendors, by design, and nothing for a cashier to learn.

What was built

  • A customer app for discovering and claiming deals as they go live through the evening.
  • A vendor console, so venues list and schedule their own stock without going through us.
  • An admin console for running the marketplace itself.
What we would tell you honestly

Luqma is our own venture, not a client engagement, which means we carry its risk and we are free to say what it has not proved yet. We are not publishing traction figures here, because a number without a denominator is decoration. What it does demonstrate is scope: a consumer app, a merchant console and an operations console, designed, shipped and kept running by the same house.

Case / 03 Under NDA In build

Technology partner to a platform business in Oman

Some companies need a platform before they need a technology department, and they are asked to make architecture and security decisions years before they have anyone qualified to make them. We act as technology advisor and build lead to a platform business in Oman: leading the development team, owning architecture and security, and delivering customer apps, an operations console and an encrypted data room.

We cannot describe their product. We can describe the discipline, and the discipline is the part that transfers.

How the engagement is run

  • Security is a named programme, not a line item. A fixed set of mandatory controls applies at every commercial tier. Tiers differ by infrastructure resilience, never by whether the data is protected, because security is the one thing that cannot be retrofitted after launch.
  • Data residency and legal responsibility are settled in writing before build. Who controls the personal data and who merely processes it is agreed at the start, so that after an incident nobody is reading a contract hoping it says something helpful.
  • Infrastructure is chosen on three year total cost, not sticker price. Two routes were fully costed, including hardware replacement on one side and price rises and exit cost on the other, before either was recommended.
  • Supplier quotations are audited line by line. On this engagement that review found arithmetic errors inside the supplier's own totals, and the quote went back before anyone signed anything.
  • Acceptance is defined as demonstrations, not deliverables. "The booking module is complete" is an argument. "A customer books, a job is completed, payment is taken" either works or it does not. Acceptance disputes are how staged contracts die.
What we would tell you honestly

This engagement is under confidentiality, so we will not name the client, the sector or the figures, and you should be suspicious of any supplier who would. References are available privately on request. The reason this work is on the page at all is the audit habit: the most valuable thing we did in the first months was not writing code, it was reading someone else's numbers carefully enough to find the mistakes in them.
